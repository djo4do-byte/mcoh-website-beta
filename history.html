Since about 1850

Our history

Among San Francisco’s oldest funeral homes, and still family-owned.

Timeline

More than a century and a half of care

Select any image to enlarge. Use arrow keys to browse; Esc to close.

  1. About 1850

    Stage and livery roots

    Our story begins in Gold Rush–era San Francisco with the Beaudry stage and livery business.

  2. 1875

    Both sides of the Bay

    As early as 1875, the firm was one of the first to offer expanded services on both sides of San Francisco Bay.

  3. The years that followed

    Flanigan & Gallagher, then McAvoy

    The business carried on as Flanigan & Gallagher, later McAvoy & Gallagher, and then McAvoy, O’Hara & Company.

  4. Dan O’Hara Sr.

    1903

    Arriving in San Francisco

    Born in County Tyrone in 1884, Daniel J. O’Hara (Dan Sr.) arrives in San Francisco.

    1904

    Joining the firm

    Dan O’Hara Sr. joins the firm, and the O’Hara family name becomes part of its future. In San Francisco, the firm builds California’s first modern funeral facility.

    1906

    Destroyed and rebuilt

    The building is destroyed in 1906 and rebuilt.

    Innovations

    A concealed choir, an electric elevator, and upstairs family rooms.

  5. 1945

    Dan O’Hara Jr.

    Dan O’Hara Jr. inherits the company in 1945 and becomes a leader of California funeral directors. He helps design the nation’s most consumer-oriented pre-arrangement legislation and a price disclosure law that becomes a model for the Federal Trade Commission Funeral Rule.

  6. 2000

    150 years recognized

    The firm reaches its 150th anniversary.

  7. 2003

    Protecting consumers

    Mayor Willie Brown recognizes Dan and Richard O’Hara for helping design a comprehensive funeral service disclosure law.

  8. 2006

    California Assembly · 2006

    Century Club certificate of recognition (Mark Leno)

  9. 2006

    City of San Francisco · 2006

    Water-bond commendation

  10. Today

    Still on Geary

    We remain family-owned: today Richard O’Hara and Dan O’Hara III, third-generation funeral directors, carry on a tradition of personal, unhurried care. They are joined by the fourth generation: Dan O’Hara IV, Olga and William O’Hara.
